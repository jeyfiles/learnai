skill: Learning plan

recallQuestion: Explain prompt injection in one sentence, without looking it up.
workedExample:
  situation: Sofia works in marketing and has done a practice most days for two weeks. She wanted a plan for next week that fits around her job.
  input: "She listed her free time, her career path and the two skills she found hardest."
  approach:
    - Free time. "20 minutes on weekday mornings, one hour on Saturday."
    - Path and weak spots. "AI powered professional. Checking sources and writing scoring rules."
    - She asked an AI tool for a five day plan, one task per day, with the Saturday hour for one portfolio piece.
    - She cut one task that did not fit her week and moved the portfolio piece to Sunday.
  output: A five line plan she pinned above her desk, with one portfolio piece to finish by the weekend.
  whyItWorks: A plan built from your real time and your real weak spots is one you will follow. Editing it yourself makes it yours.
task:
  outcome: A plan for next week, one small task per day, built around your real time and weak spots.
  steps:
    - Write down how much time you really have on each day next week.
    - Name your career path and the two skills from these practices you found hardest.
    - Ask an AI tool for a plan, using the prompt below.
    - Remove or move anything that does not fit, then save the plan where you will see it.
  prompt: "I am working towards a career as [path]. Next week I have [time per day]. The skills I find hardest are [skill 1] and [skill 2]. Make a plan with one small task per day and one portfolio piece to finish by the weekend."
successChecks:
  - The plan fits the time you actually have.
  - It includes practice on your two weakest skills.
  - It ends with one portfolio piece you can show someone.
ifStuck: If the plan feels too full, halve it. Doing a small plan every day is better than a big plan once.
