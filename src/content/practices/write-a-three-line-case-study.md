---
title: Write a three line case study
order: 9
minutes: 20
skill: Portfolio
stage: independent
paths: [all]
recallQuestion: Why is it useful to ask for the exact source line next to each point in a summary?
workedExample:
  situation: Hana finished a lesson where she compared two AI tools for a work task. She wanted to add it to her portfolio.
  input: "Problem, what I did, result. Three lines."
  approach:
    - Problem. "Our team spent two hours a week writing meeting notes by hand."
    - What I did. "I tested two AI tools on the same three meetings and scored them on accuracy, action items and length."
    - Result. "The better tool saved about 90 minutes a week, and I wrote a checklist so every summary is checked before it is shared."
  output: A three line case study with a real number and a link to her score sheet.
  whyItWorks: Hiring managers read fast. Three lines with a problem, an action and a result show what you can do better than a list of tools.
task:
  outcome: A three line case study for one thing you made with AI, ready for your portfolio page.
  steps:
    - Choose one piece of work you finished in a lesson, a workbook or your job.
    - Write the problem, what you did and the result, one line each.
    - Add one number if you honestly have one, and a link to the proof.
    - Ask an AI tool to point out anything vague, then fix it yourself.
  prompt: "Here is a three line case study for my portfolio. Point out any words that are vague or any claim a hiring manager might doubt. Do not rewrite it. [paste your three lines]"
successChecks:
  - Each of the three lines does one job. Problem, action, result.
  - Every claim is true and you can show proof for it.
  - Someone outside your field could understand it.
ifStuck: If you have no number, describe the change instead, for example "the team now uses this checklist every week".
